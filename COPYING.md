## What This Licence Means in Practice

Because the licence is permissive, you may fork Fed-Poster, modify it, bundle it into a commercial product, rebrand it, and even distribute it under a different name — all without asking permission and without releasing your own modifications back to the community. The only obligations are to retain the copyright and permission notice. There is no copyleft requirement and no patent retaliation clause (contrast with Apache-2.0 or the GPL family).

This permissiveness is intentional and aligns with the project's commercial model: the open-source core stays free and fully functional, while the hosted commercial tiers described in [`PRICING.md`](PRICING.md) — including the Agency tier's white-label rights — are delivered as services and contracts layered on top of the same MIT-licensed code rather than as licence restrictions. ## Third-Party Components

Fed-Poster loads a small number of third-party assets from public CDNs at runtime. These are not distributed in this repository but are referenced by the HTML:

- **Inter** typeface, by Rasmus Andersson, licensed under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. Loaded from Google Fonts.
- **Font Awesome 6.5** icons, by Fonticons, Inc., loaded from cdnjs under the Font Awesome Free License (icons: CC BY 4.0; code: MIT).
- **Ko-fi** overlay widget scripts, loaded from Ko-fi's CDN per their widget integration terms.

No third-party JavaScript libraries are vendored into the repository. The project intentionally avoids runtime dependencies that would enlarge the attack surface or violate the no-telemetry stance described in [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md) and ADR-001.
